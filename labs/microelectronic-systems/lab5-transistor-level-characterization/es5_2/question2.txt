Varying the load from 0.05 fF to 50 fF changes how the gate behaves over time: at 50 fF the current needed to charge and discharge 
the load gets much bigger and lasts longer. This makes the output take longer to change with a delay of 209.0 ps for a High-to-Low 
signal and a fall time of 296.5 ps. During this change the current going to ground helps get rid of the stored charge in the capacitor.
The current surge is very high because it has to discharge the capacitors. At the time the PMOS transistors stay partially on for a longer time and his 
increases the short-circuit current, so it makes the supply current wider peaking at around -70 uA.
When the load is small at 0.05 fF, short-circuit current is the main issue. With a 50 fF load moving the charge becomes the main focus causing 
longer conduction and bigger current peaks. The loads charge and discharge dictate the energy usage with current peaks, across the power rails.
