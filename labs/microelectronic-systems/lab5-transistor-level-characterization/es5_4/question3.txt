Increasing the gate size from HS X1 to HS X8 under a 50 fF load creates a big speed-power trade-off, because the transistor channel 
width increases by 8 times and the channel resistance (RON) decreases by 8 times. This helps the gate charge and discharge the large load capacitance 
much faster. The propagation delay (tpHL) drops from 209.0 ps to 66.8 ps. The fall time gets sharper from 296.5 ps to 100.8 ps.
The rise time also drops, from 363.9 ps to 105.4 ps. However there is a downside to this change: it uses a lot silicon area and power.
The supply current peak (IVDD) increases from around -70 uA to around -400 uA, because of dynamic and short-circuit currents.
Also the larger X8 layout has an input gate capacitance, which adds a load to the previous logic stage. As a result its propagation delay increases.
The gate sizing optimization helps with speed, but it increases power dissipation and silicon area, so the trade-off is between speed and power.
The HS X8 gate configuration provides better performance, using more power and silicon area.
