Question 1
Analyze the real timing report. Did something change? Do you understand the meaning of the reported values?

The first report refers to a simpler combinational path, whereas the second is associated with synchronous
sequential logic and therefore must wait for the clock signal.
For the matter of the FF-D it doesn't change much (0.10 - 0.07), but the synchronous one introduce some AOI
and OAI logic after the fact that probably is the I/O logic.
The slack is valued only in the synchronous case, and it is calculated from the clock duration, but in both
cases the critical path is faster than the clock of an order of magnitude.

Question 2
Consider again Figure 3.2, what happens to the combinational paths between the inputs INs and the first stage
of registers (paths in Block 1), or between the output registers and the outputs OUTs (paths in block 3)?

The `create_clock` command only sets timing for the logic that works between two FF-D and not for the I/O circuit,
so it is only applied to block 4 (between two FF), while block 1 (input to FF) and block 3 (FF to output) are uneffected.
To make these paths better you need to add limits, like `set_max_delay`, to include these paths in the optimization.

Question 3
How does the timing report change with respect to the one obtained from the previous structure?

The timing report shows that the windowed Register File architecture is more complex and introduces higher delays
compared to the behavioral version:
The critical path now starts from the CWP register, meaning the slowest operation is the address translation logic
rather than simple data access.
The total delay increases to 0.96 ns due to additional combinational logic, and that reduces the slack down to 1.00.
The logic depth is greater, with more layers of gates needed for circular buffer management and window selection.
